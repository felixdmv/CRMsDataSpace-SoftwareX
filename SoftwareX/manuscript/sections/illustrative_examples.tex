\section{Illustrative case study and empirical evaluation}
\label{sec:IllustrativeExamples}

This section demonstrates the functional execution of the proposed software architecture through an illustrative case study within the European Critical Raw Materials domain (\textbf{CRMsDataSpace}) and presents an extensive empirical evaluation comparing the architecture against alternative natural language processing baselines.

\subsection{Step-by-Step Illustrative Walkthrough}
\label{subsec:Walkthrough}

To demonstrate how the architecture translates natural language queries into synchronized cartographic exploration, consider a user executing a multi-criteria spatial query:
\begin{quote}
\textit{``Muestra escombreras de litio y cobalto en España y Finlandia que estén activas''} \\
\small(\textit{``Show active lithium and cobalt waste dumps in Spain and Finland''})
\end{quote}

The execution proceeds through the four pipeline stages:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Conversational NLU Extraction}: The NLU module evaluates the query against the OpenAPI schema under greedy decoding. It outputs a validated JSON intent payload:
    \begin{verbatim}
    {
      "intent": "search",
      "countries": ["spain", "finland"],
      "commodities": ["lithium", "cobalt"],
      "storage_facility_types": ["waste_dump"],
      "project_status": ["active"]
    }
    \end{verbatim}
    \item \textbf{Canonicalization and Solr Query Generation}: Component~2 normalizes entities and constructs the parameterized filter query array $F_q$:
    \begin{verbatim}
    fq=country_canonical_s:("spain" OR "finland")
    fq=commodities_ss:("lithium" OR "cobalt")
    fq=facility_type_s:("waste_dump")
    fq=status_s:("active")
    \end{verbatim}
    \item \textbf{Spatial Search Execution}: The Solr spatial core executes the filters, returning matching facility records along with geographic coordinates, commodity grades, and facet distributions.
    \item \textbf{GIS Map Synchronization}: The Leaflet web client receives the search response and triggers three simultaneous UI updates:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Renders active filter badges above the map: \texttt{[Country: Spain, Finland]}, \texttt{[Commodity: Lithium, Cobalt]}, \texttt{[Facility: Waste Dump]}, \texttt{[Status: Active]}.
        \item Re-centers the map to frame the filtered European bounding box, activating glowing CSS pulse rings on matching facilities while dimming non-matching sites.
        \item Updates the site counter to reflect the active subset (e.g., \textit{``Showing 6 / 100 European Sites''}) and generates a grounded conversational summary.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

A second challenging scenario illustrates the system's handling of environmental risk criteria and negative/exclusion conditions:
\begin{quote}
\textit{``Balsas de wolframio sin restaurar en Alemania''} \\
\small(\textit{``Unrestored tungsten tailings ponds in Germany''})
\end{quote}
Here, the NLU engine extracts \texttt{"storage\_facility\_types": ["tailings\_pond"]}, \texttt{"commodities": ["tungsten"]}, \texttt{"countries": ["germany"]}, and explicitly assigns the boolean constraint \texttt{"restored": false}. The system filters out restored facilities and highlights vulnerable unrestored tailings impoundments across Germany for environmental risk auditing.

\subsection{Empirical Comparison with NLP and Search Baselines}
\label{subsec:BaselineComparison}

To validate the architectural superiority of the proposed decoupled NLU-Solr pipeline, we conducted an empirical benchmark across four competing paradigms evaluated over 40 critical stress test cases covering typos, complex multi-hop entities, and domain slang:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Solr-Direct / Heuristic Rules (Legacy)}: Keyword matching and regular expressions without semantic normalization.
    \item \textbf{spaCy NLP Pipeline}: Classical pipeline using the Spanish model \texttt{es\_core\_news\_sm} augmented with custom \texttt{EntityRuler} patterns~\cite{spacy2020}.
    \item \textbf{GLiNER Zero-Shot Model}: Generalist transformer-based entity extraction model (\texttt{gliner\_small-v2.1}) evaluated zero-shot~\cite{zaratiana2024gliner}.
    \item \textbf{Proposed NLU-Solr Architecture}: Dual-variant Few-Shot NLU with OpenAPI JSON Schema enforcement coupled to canonical normalizers.
\end{enumerate}

\centering
\small
\caption{Empirical benchmark comparison across 40 critical stress test cases in the validation testbed.}
\label{tab:paradigms_benchmark}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\hline
\textbf{Architecture Paradigm} & \textbf{Extraction Accuracy (\%)} & \textbf{Mean Latency} & \textbf{Robustness to Slang \& Typos} \\
\hline
Solr-Direct / Heuristics & 48.7\% & 0.2 ms & Very Low (Fails on synonyms) \\
GLiNER Zero-Shot & 16.9\% & 91.5 ms & Low (Cannot generalize domain terms) \\
spaCy NLP Pipeline & 71.9\% & 11.7 ms & Moderate (Sensitive to inflection) \\
\textbf{Proposed NLU-Solr Architecture} & \textbf{96.6\%} & \textbf{1.1 ms*} & \textbf{Excellent (Deep semantic mapping)} \\

As presented in Table~\ref{tab:paradigms_benchmark}, rule-based heuristics achieve only 48.7\% accuracy, failing whenever users utilize non-canonical terminology. spaCy reaches 71.9\% but stumbles on complex multi-word facility definitions, while GLiNER fails to generalize to domain vocabulary zero-shot (16.9\%). In contrast, the proposed architecture achieves \textbf{96.6\% accuracy}, demonstrating exceptional robustness.

The evaluations were performed directly on an institutional High-Performance Computing (HPC) node provisioned through the Slurm workload manager, equipped with:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Accelerator}: $1\times$ NVIDIA A100-PCIE-40GB GPU (40~GB HBM2e memory, 1,555~GB/s memory bandwidth).
    \item \textbf{Host Compute}: Dual Intel Xeon Gold processors (2.40~GHz, 32 physical cores), 256~GB DDR4 system memory.
    \item \textbf{Software Stack}: Linux (Red Hat / CentOS ecosystem), Python 3.12, PyTorch with CUDA~12 support, and Hugging Face Transformers executing under half-precision (FP16).
\end{itemize}

All models were evaluated across the 100-query benchmark battery (\texttt{test\_battery\_100.json}) using identical prompt templates and greedy decoding ($T = 0.0$). The empirical findings yield critical technical insights for the deployment of conversational spatial search:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Guaranteed Data Sovereignty Without Accuracy Compromise}: All evaluated on-premises models achieve Macro F1-scores exceeding 90--93.4\%, matching or outperforming commercial cloud APIs (Gemini 1.5 Flash at 88.3\%). Crucially, geographic boundaries and strategic CRM metals reach 100.0\% F1 across Llama 3.2, Qwen 2.5, and DeepSeek R1. This confirms that organizations handling strategic mineral inventories, cadastral databases, or defense-sensitive assets can fully eliminate external cloud API subscriptions without sacrificing extraction fidelity.
    \item \textbf{Hardware Efficiency and Departmental Feasibility}: The ultra-compact \textbf{Llama 3.2 3B} consumes only 6.0~GB of VRAM, demonstrating that conversational spatial interfaces do not require massive enterprise data centers and can operate on departmental or mid-range workstations equipped with standard 8~GB or 12~GB GPUs (e.g., NVIDIA RTX 3080/4070).
    \item \textbf{High-Capacity Spatial Reasoning}: The 7-billion parameter architectures (\textbf{Qwen 2.5 7B} and \textbf{DeepSeek R1 7B}) require 14.2~GB of VRAM, utilizing only 35.5\% of the A100's 40~GB capacity. Their higher capacity yields superior disambiguation of complex cross-border criteria (e.g., distinguishing unexploited raw material deposits from secondary extractive waste impoundments) and achieves the highest overall exact match rate (64--65\%).
    \item \textbf{Deterministic Baseline Complementarity}: The rule-based mock engine executes in 0.3~ms on CPU with 94.2\% Macro F1, providing an ultra-responsive fallback that guarantees zero-latency operation during network outages or GPU maintenance windows.
\end{enumerate}
